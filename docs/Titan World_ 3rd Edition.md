# Introduction

*PATHS* connect all subjects of Ymir. They are the lifeblood of the Eldian race, and transcend time and space. *PATHS* are what brought forth the Titans, hundreds of years ago. A young girl, named Ymir, came into contact with the source of all life on earth, and was granted immense power. The power of the Titans.  
With this power, however, came a great burden onto humanity. As science advanced, and Ymir died, her power was split three times among her children, and then each three times more. With the powers of human advancement, people discovered that the descendants of Ymir had a latent ability within them. When given the proper mixture of drugs, injected into the spine, they would become a Titan.  
These man-made Titans were horrific creatures, which were mindless and suffering. Their only motivation and behaviour was to devour any Eldians nearby, in hopes of becoming a human once more. When a Titan ate a “Titan Shifter”, a human with a fraction of Ymir’s power, it would regain its human form, and the powers of the Titan shifter before it. Eventually, this “Titan Serum” was used both as a punishment for those that opposed the people with it, and as a tool to transfer the powers of Titan Shifters between holders.  
Of the nine Titan powers, the fractions of Ymir, one of them remained the most powerful, that being the Founding Titan. The Founding Titan was the Titan power that was closest to Ymir’s original form, and had the most power out of any other Titan. It could control Eldians, including the mindless Titans. This Titan’s power, however, came with two caveats. The first one being that, if the holder of the Founder was not a member of Ymir’s royal family, and was not touching a Titan of royal blood, they could not use the Founder’s power in any way.  
The second caveat was that the king of Eldia made an unbreakable vow, one of nonhostility. This meant that any descendants of that king, Eldians of royal blood, could never use the Founder’s power as an act of war. This means that, to use the Founder to its full potential, it requires the cooperation of a Royal Titan, and a man of common blood, to truly unleash its wrath.  
This unbreakable vow was brought after Eldia used its powers of Titan shifting for years, to oppress the world. Through advancements in society, the Titans were defeated by the combined forces of the nation of Marley and her allies. They banished the Eldians to an island, known as Paradis, and sent all of their converted mindless Titans there. This forced the King to create three walls, named after the original daughter of Ymir.   
These walls, Maria, Rosa, and Sina, were formed from the hardened skin of Colossal Titans, made to remain in place for a hundred years, where they remain to this day, trapping humanity inside. Most people of Eldia are unaware of the existence of Titans Shifters, or their former empire. They were taught to believe that Eldia was the final bastion of humanity, and that Titans had ravaged all of the world beyond the walls.  
As it currently stands, Eldia only contains one of the 9 Titan powers, that being the founder. It’s a closely guarded secret, kept by the royal family, and passed down the generations.

# Author’s Notes and Preface

*Titan World 3rd Edition* is a completely fan made project, made by a guy who just loves the game. It’s not made by the guy that made the first two editions, and it’s not associated with the official *Attack on Titan* brand.  
While this rulebook does explain a decent amount of the lore of Attack on Titan, it is still recommended that you’ve seen at least the first season of the show before playing.

# RPG Terminology

Titan World is a Roleplaying-Game. This means you create a character, and step into their shoes, act as they would act. It’s sort of like a war reenactment, with the mechanics and rules to keep the game fair like you would there, all at the comfort of your table. There are many famous role playing games out there, such as *Call of Cthulhu*, *Cyberpunk 2020*, and of course, *Dungeons & Dragons*.

## Players and Game Masters

In Titan World, there are two types of players. There are the *players*, the ones creating characters and going on an adventure, and the *GM*, or Game Master, the one who runs the game, and decides what happens.  
As the **Game Master** of a game of Titan World, you play the game very differently from the other players. Instead of creating and controlling a character, you control the world itself. It’s your job to create a mission and scenario for the players, and control the *NPCs* (Non-Player Characters) and enemies. Your fun is just as important as the players’, but it’s important that you don’t overshadow the importance of their characters with your own NPCs.  
As the GM, you’re the one who decides what a player can and cannot do. If a player wants their character to do something, but you figure that it would be outright impossible in that situation, you have every right to say no.

## Rolling

As a **player** in this game, you get as much freedom as your character. This means that, while you don’t get to decide exactly what happens, you get to decide what you try to do. This is represented through two dice, which are rolled every time you want to try to do something that would be difficult. When rolling your dice, the results are as follows.

| On a 10+ | You succeed in what you were trying to do. |
| :---- | :---- |
| **On a 7-9** | You’re a step closer to success, but something went wrong to hinder you or slow you down.. |
| **On a 6 or Less** | You fail at what you were trying to do, and need to find a new approach before you can try again. |

A roll of snake eyes (two ones) is always a failure, and two sixes is always the best possible outcome.  
If you’re playing as the GM, rolls are specifically your call. A player doesn’t decide when to roll or not, you always ask the player to roll. If you want a player to succeed at something, or fail something, there’s no point in asking them to roll, and it should only be done when it’s necessary.

## Difficult Rolls

Sometimes a player wants to do something exceptionally difficult. In a situation like this, it’s best to change the above table slightly, so that on any roll lower than a 10 is considered to be a complete failure.

## Advantage

When a player has a reason, at the GM’s discretion, to have better chances of completing a task, they can gain a level of *advantage*. Advantage will give a player an additional number added to their roll, so if they roll a 6, the roll will instead be a 7\. If they roll a 9, the roll will instead be a 10\. Advantage can be stacked up to 3 times (resulting in a \+3), at the GM’s discretion.

# Characters & Character Creation

***“As we ride to certain death, we trust our successors to do the same for us.” \- Erwin Smith***  
As a player in Titan World, the way you interact with the world presented to you is through a character. A person within this world that you take control of. These characters consist of several attributes, the main ones being your *Statistics*, or stats.  
In Titan World, there are several stats.

| Agility | Your speed and precision. Used for everything from catching a ball, to zipping away with your ODM gear. |
| :---: | :---- |
| **Technique** | Your skill and effectiveness with a weapon. For everything from firing a rifle at a target far away, to stabbing someone up close. |
| **Mind** | How well you perceive the world around you, and how good you are at applying that information. For everything from listening in on a conversation, to formulating a plan, to patching up your allies. |
| **Body** | Just how tough you are. It’s how big you are, and how much of a beating you can give. Used for how much you can dish out, and how much you can take. |
| **Heart** | How long you can keep going before you give up. Not for your body’s physical extremes, but your mind’s. With enough heart, you can stare death in the face and not bat an eye. |
| **Duty** | Your worth as a soldier. It determines how much people respect you, and how much they’re willing to do what you say. |

## Assigning Stats

When creating your character, you’re generally going to be at a different level of skill depending on the kind of game you’re playing in. Your GM will start you off with one of the following packages, and you can assign the given numbers accordingly.

| Rookie | 1, 1, 0, 0, \-1, \-1 |
| :---: | :---: |
| **Soldier** | **2, 1, 1, 0, 0, \-1** |
| **Expert** | **2, 1, 1, 1, 0, \-1 OR 3, 2, 1, 0, \-1, \-1** |

When assigning your stats, think about the character that you want to play. What do you want your role to be in your group? What do you want to excel at, and what are you okay with not being so great at? Questions like these not only help you establish how your character will be from a gameplay perspective, but also allow you to come up with more personality and backstory, as you wonder *why* your character has the skills that they do.

## Background

A character is meant to be a person that truly exists within the world of Attack on Titan. Because of this, they obviously didn’t just spring up out of nowhere. Where did your character come from, and how did they get to the point where they are?  
A good character background contains 3 main points. Details, an anchor, and a drive.

### *Details*

The details of your backstory, also known as the “fluff” is the part where you really get to let your creativity shine. Where is your character from, and what was life like there? This is the part where it’s especially recommended to work together with your GM, and establish parts of the world to really have your character feel like a part of it.

### *Anchor*

An *anchor* is what stops your character from just running headfirst into death’s arms to achieve their goal. It’s something waiting for them to go back home to, and a reason to want to stay alive. It could be anything from a family, to a life of luxury, to even just a really nice dog. This part is important, because it’s what stops your character from just giving up.

### *Drive*

A *drive* is what motivates your character to *leave* their anchor. It’s their reason for not just enjoying a cushy life at home, and instead actually putting their life at risk. It could be anything from poverty, to pride, or a hatred for Titans. What’s important about a drive, is that it’s the only thing keeping your character from just staying home instead of going on a journey.

## Post-Game Rewards

While there is no explicit “level” system in Titan World, you do gain certain benefits, at the GM’s discretion. When you’ve reached a point of growth in some way, you can choose from the following.

* Increase any one of your stats. Every stat can only be increased once, and a stat of 3 cannot be increased any higher.  
* Receive a Military Promotion of some sort, to a limit at your GM’s discretion.  
* Reattribute your stats, at the cost of any previous increases you may have gotten.  
* Receive extra gear or reinforcements on your next mission.

# Staying Alive, and Also The Opposite

***“If you win you live. If you lose you die. If you don’t fight, you can’t win.” – Eren Jaeger***  
In Titan World, you are nothing. At least, that’s how it might feel, if your GM is feeling especially cruel. For the most part, you’re only human, and staying alive tends to be a top priority.

## Injuries

You’ve got 6 main parts to you. Your head, your chest, your arms, and your legs. They can get scuffed up a little, or they can get *seriously* hurt. In Titan World, there are 3 categories of injuries.

| Minor | Cuts and nasty bruises, things that aren’t gonna slow you down too much, but they could get worse. |
| :---: | :---- |
| **Major** | Things like broken bones, and awful bleeding. This is gonna put you out of commission if you’re not quick to treat it. Major wounds to the arms make your Technique stat one point lower, major wounds to the legs make your Agility one point lower, major wounds to the head make your Mind one point lower, and major wounds to the chest make your Body one point lower. These may change at the GM’s discretion depending on the situation. |
| **Crippling** | These injuries are permanent, and they’re gonna be slowing you down for the rest of your life.  They make checks difficult as a major wound. Consciousness lost from a crippling wound cannot be regained until you have spent a night to rest, and you cannot rest until a crippling wound has been amputated and treated. |

### *Consciousness*

These injuries can be acquired through all sorts of means, like getting beat up, screwing up an ODM roll, or getting swatted out of the air by a Titan. Injuries have all sorts of different effects on you, and affect your ***Consciousness***. Consciousness is a derived stat, made up of **3 \+ Body \+ Heart**, and it determines exactly what it sounds like.  
Every major injury you receive takes 1 off of your consciousness, except for major injuries to the head, which take 2 off of your consciousness instead. If your consciousness falls to 0, you’re incapacitated. While incapacitated, you’re unable to speak, move, or act in any way, as you are unconscious. Injuries are given at the GM’s discretion for the most part, of how severe a certain circumstance would be.  
When incapacitated, depending on your injuries, you can regain 1 point of consciousness and wake up if an appropriate amount of time has passed where you have not received any damage, at the GM’s discretion.

#### ***Comas***

If, through a crippling wound, your maximum consciousness is reduced to a 0 or lower, you fall into a coma. Unless you are a Titan Shifter, your crippling wound cannot be healed, meaning you are effectively in a vegetative state.

### *Suffocation*

When suffocating, **Roll \+ Body**. On a 9 or lower, you lose one point of consciousness. Titans are immune to suffocation. These are regained once you have a minute to catch your breath.

### *The Four Types of Injuries*

Earlier, we discussed the three categories of injuries, but there are different ways of getting injured. There are four types of injuries in Titan World, blunt injuries, cutting injuries, piercing/ballistic injuries, and burning injuries. If a character is injured in any other way, use your own discretion.

| Cut | When a cut is a minor injury, any rolls on cutting attacks towards the same limb have advantage, for each existing cut. When a cut is a major injury, you will steadily lose points of consciousness, at the GM’s discretion. Bleeding will have a cap of however many major cut wounds are present. At 1-4 wounds, there is a cap of \-1 consciousness from bleeding, and at 5, you can die from bleeding. When a cut is a crippling injury, that limb is completely severed. Crippling cuts can also deal lethal bleeding. A crippling cutting wound to the head is an instant death. |
| :---: | :---- |
| **Blunt** | When you receive a minor blunt wound, any rolls on blunt attacks towards the same limb have advantage, for each existing bruise or fracture. When a blunt wound is a major injury, the bone is considered to be broken, and must be set with a splint or cast to be treated. When a blunt wound is a crippling injury on one of the 4 limbs, that limb’s bone is completely shattered, and must be amputated. When a blunt wound is a crippling injury on the chest, you receive a spinal injury, and are crippled from the waist down. When a blunt wound is a crippling injury on the head, you receive permanent brain damage, reducing your “Mind” stat to \-3, which cannot be raised any higher. |
| **Piercing (Ballistic)** | When a piercing injury is a minor injury, it has the same stat check debuffs as a major injury. When a piercing injury is a major injury, the wound bleeds similar to a major cutting injury. When a piercing injury is a crippling injury, it either completely shatters a bone beyond repair, or destroys an organ inside the body. A crippling piercing wound to the head will instantly kill the target, and a major wound will blast a hole through their jaw in some way, preventing them from speaking. |
|  **Burn** | When a burn is a minor injury, any rolls against being burned are difficult, for each existing burn. Three minor burn wounds will automatically become a major wound. When a burn is a major injury, you must pass a **Roll+Body** or begin to bleed. When a burn is a crippling injury, the limb is completely charred and dehydrated, and essentially unusable. It must be amputated to function. A crippling burn  wound to the chest or head is untreatable, and will result in a slow and certain death. |

### *Healing Injuries*

When performing first-aid on yourself or someone else, you can attempt to turn a Major Wound into a Minor Wound. **Roll \+ Mind**. On a 7-9, the wound is not healed, but you manage to stop bleeding from a non-crippling wound. On a 6 or lower, the target loses one point of consciousness, and bleeding from a major cutting wound loses the limit on the amount of consciousness it can lower someone to, meaning it can be lethal. If two ones are rolled while attempting first-aid, the major wound becomes crippling. On a 10+, the major wound becomes a minor wound, and consciousness lost is restored. First-aid can be performed on a minor piercing wound, and on a 10+ it is replaced with a minor cutting wound.  
**Minor wounds gained from a healed major wound should be marked as such on the character sheet.**  
If you are performing first-aid with bandages, you recieve a \+1. Otherwise, you must use something like a piece of your uniform as a makeshift bandage to close a cut or set a bone. When performing first-aid with a full first-aid kit, it has 3 uses, and gives a **\+2 Advantage** when used.  
Minor wounds will heal slowly on their own over time. When you’ve had a moment to rest, **Roll \+ Body**. On a 10+, one minor wound (Not piercing)  of the GM’s choosing can be removed. Minor wounds that are the result of a major wound being healed cannot be removed until your mission is over. Alternatively, instead of removing a minor wound, the GM can restore a point of consciousness lost through fighting, bleeding, or other factors not related to a major wound.  
Crippling wounds cannot be properly healed, however crippling cutting  can be mitigated. When performing first-aid on a crippling cutting wound, **Roll \+ Mind** with the same rules as above. On a 10+, the limb is still gone, however the bleeding has been stopped, and consciousness lost can be restored in a similar manner to a minor wound healing.

## Death

On a long enough timeline, the survival rate for everyone drops to zero. If your game lasts long enough, you *will* die. There are three kinds of death. Mechanical deaths, narrative deaths, and Titan deaths.  
A mechanical death is as the name implies. It’s when you die through game mechanics, and injuries. If you lose twice your maximum consciousness, you die. There’s nothing that can be done about it, you’re just dead.  
A narrative death is one where there are no game mechanics at play, but you’d reasonably be dead. Examples for this are mainly being executed, such as having your throat cut or getting shot in the head at point blank.  
The third, and final type of death, is a Titan death. If you are eaten by a Titan, and you are bitten in half, *you are dead.* Regardless of if you’re a shifter or not, you die, then and there.  
When you’re dead, that’s it. You have to make a new character, who will eventually die too. The world of Titans is a cruel one, and you’re going to die. Keep that in mind, and don’t get upset when you do.

## Explosives

Generally, things that go boom are really good at hurting things. In Titan World, there are many ways of accomplishing this. Most military encampments have some form of dynamite, and a gas canister will explode when shot.  
When throwing an explosive, **Roll \+ Technique**. On a 7-9, you don’t hit your target dead-on, but it will likely be caught in the blast. If your target is a person, they gain a \+1 advantage to escaping the blast. On a 10+, your target is hit by the explosive itself, on a 6 or lower you miss your target altogether, and on a roll of two ones, your explosive detonates in your hand.  
If your explosive of choice is a canister of gas, someone else must **Roll \+ Technique** to shoot it at the right time. On a 9 or lower, you miss the target, and it doesn’t detonate.  
Titan Transformations, and point blank shots of a flare gun, are considered to be explosions.

### *Getting Caught in The Blast*

When an explosive is about to go off next to you, **Roll \+ Agility**. On a 6 or lower, you are completely caught in the blast, and immediately take a major burn wound on every limb. **Roll \+ Body**, on a 6 or lower, you take a crippling wound on one or more limbs at the GM’s discretion.  
On a 7-9 for your agility roll, only a single limb is caught in the blast. **Roll \+ Body**. On a 9 or lower, you take a major burn wound on that limb, and on a 10+, you take a minor wound. On a roll of two ones, you take a crippling wound on that limb.  
On a 10+ for your agility roll, you completely escape the blast, and are only knocked prone.

### *Using Explosives on Titans*

If you successfully roll on throwing an explosive at a Titan, you can incapacitate whichever limb you are targeting. If you are attempting to throw an explosive at the weak point of a Titan, the regular rules apply, and it must be distracted or incapacitated if you do not have an Agility and Technique of \+3, along with rolling a 10+.

## Titans

Titans, the mindless kind, are hulking monstrosities that only exist to consume. In the second edition, Titans were not much of a threat. Here, that has changed. No longer will characters be on the level of humanity's greatest heroes, not without earning it.

### *Titan Size*

Titans range anywhere from 3 metres, all the way to 15 metres in height. They’re surprisingly light for their weight, and the average man could likely lift a 3 metre Titan if he tried. If a Titan’s height is within 2 metres of your own height, you may engage it in hand-to-hand combat, which will be elaborated on further in this rulebook. This height range of 2 metres is increased if you are Titan-sized.   
If you are Titan Sized (10 metres or taller), you can fight any Titan within 10 metres of your own height. If the Titan is at least 5 metres taller than you, however, every roll against it is difficult.  
If a Titan is within your hand-to-hand range, then decapitating it with a crippling wound to the head will be enough to kill it, as it would likely do enough damage to destroy its nape as well.  
When engaging in Hand-To-Hand combat with a Titan, you will immediately fail any and all rolls against your Titan opponent, if you are not a Titan yourself, or you do not have a Body stat of at least \+2. Titans are always assumed to have a Body stat of \+3, including shifters.  
Note that mindless Titans cannot be knocked unconscious. Instead of reducing a Titan’s consciousness to the negatives in hand-to-hand, you must deliver a crippling blow to its head. Consciousness still applies to Titan Shifters, and is representative of them losing the energy to maintain their Titan form.  
If you receive a striking blow from a Titan that is not within your melee height range, and you are not a Titan yourself, then you are likely to be horribly injured. If the Titan rolls a 10+, and your body stat is lower than \+2, then you are killed. If your body stat is a \+2 or higher, or the Titan rolls a 7-9, then you take a crippling blunt injury to the chest. If the titan rolls a 6 or lower, but still beats your roll to dodge, you take a major blunt wound to the chest.

### *Titan Injuries*

Titans are incredibly resilient creatures, with a logic-defying healing factor. Because of this, they heal minor injuries in a matter of seconds, and major injuries in a matter of minutes. Regardless of this, they are still affected by those injuries while they last. If a Titan’s size is not within the hand-to-hand range of whatever is trying to attack it, all injuries that are not done with Titan-Slaying weapons are considered to be minor by default. Titans will also immediately heal from bleeding.  
A major wound to a Titan’s arm will cause it to drop what it is holding, and a major wound to a Titan’s leg will slow it down tremendously, unless it’s an incapacitating strike, in which case they will fall to the ground. A major wound to the head can either blind it, making it unable to see anything until it heals, or force open its jaw, preventing it from biting anything until it heals.

### *Surviving a Titan’s Grasp*

When encountering a Titan, they’ll often try to grab the nearest human. If they’re outside of your hand-to-hand size range, they’ll simply pick you up. Of course, this can be avoided with a good enough **Roll \+ Agility**. However, after a successful roll to dodge away from a Titan, you’ll receive a \-1 to each subsequent Agility check until you can catch your breath.  
 There are two ways to go about trying to escape a Titan’s grasp, pure strength, or slicing yourself free. If you’re skilled enough with your blades, and have a free hand, you can make a difficult **Roll \+ Technique** to slice open the Titan’s hand, and escape free. If that’s not an option, you can make a difficult **Roll \+ Body** to try to pry yourself out of the Titan’s grasp, though this takes long enough that your gear will be destroyed by the time you’ve made your way out, crushed by the Titan’s grasp. While in a Titan’s grasp, it is important for the GM to remember that a roll of 6 or lower is a failure in which the player must find a new approach to roll again. This means that the player *cannot* just continuously roll until they are free after failing.

### *Not Surviving a Titan’s Grasp*

Once a player has had a chance to try and free themselves from a Titan’s grasp, all players who wish to intervene must **Roll \+ Agility**. On a 6 or lower, they cannot reach the person in time. On a 7-9, they can intervene, but must act immediately. On a 10+, they may deal with something else before intervening with the Titan grabbing the player.  
If the player has failed every attempt at escaping, and no players were fast enough to intervene, the Titan eats the player, and they die.

### *Titan Bodies*

When a Titan dies, its corpse will slowly evaporate. Anyone in the way of a falling Titan, from death or a major wound to the leg, must **Roll \+ Agility**. On a 7-9, a limb other than the chest is pinned under the Titan. On a 6 or lower, their entire body is trapped underneath and their consciousness is reduced to zero (For wound purposes, counted as the chest.) On a 10+, they successfully evade the body.  
If crushed by a Titan, do a difficult  **Roll \+ Body**. On a 9 or lower, take a major blunt wound to the appropriate limb. If the Titan is dead, after each time a  player acts, **Roll \+ Body**. On a 6 or lower, you take a major burn wound on the appropriate limb, and on a 7-9, you take a minor burn wound.  
When lifting a Titan off of a comrade, **Roll \+ Body**. On a 7-9, you manage to free a single limb. On a 10+, you completely free your comrade. On a 6 or lower, you’re not able to do it, and cannot try again. If you and another comrade have both rolled 6 on trying to lift a Titan, you may try to work together. All  players **Roll \+ Body**, and then take the average. Add each result, and divide it by the number of players attempting to lift the Titan, rounding down. This may be repeated until successful. If the player trapped underneath has taken one burn wound or more, players attempting to lift the Titan must **Roll \+ Body**. On a 9 or lower, they take a minor burn wound on both arms.  
Depending on the level of evaporation of the Titan, players trying to lift it  may receive an advantage, at the GM’s discretion.

### *Killing Titans*

One thing is true about Titans, and that is that Titans have one true weak spot, being the nape on the back of their neck. Another thing is true about Titans, and that is that nothing as large as a Titan should move as fast as a Titan does. If you try to leap straight for the kill, you will most likely die.  
Striking to kill involves positioning yourself behind a Titan, and then **Roll \+ Agility** to line yourself up for a kill. On a 10+, you gain advantage for the following roll, and can move again immediately after killing the Titan, before anything else can react. On a 7-9, you line yourself up successfully, but cannot move right away. A player cannot kill a Titan that has not been incapacitated or confused, unless they have an agility of \+3, in which case they must roll a 10+ on their initial agility roll.  
After this roll, you must **Roll \+ Technique** to properly attack. On a 7-9, either you fail to kill the titan, but scrape off enough skin for someone to try again, or you successfully kill the Titan, and lose your blades, at the GM’s discretion. If it is the former, you are no longer able to attack, as the Titan has noticed you and turned around, but anyone else who is in a position to attack gains advantage to their **Roll \+ Technique**, though they must still successfully **Roll \+ Agility** like a normal attack.  
If a Titan is not confused, or incapacitated, it will always be able to turn around to avoid a killing strike. If you have made a move to incapacitate a Titan, such as by blinding it, or slashing at its legs, you must **Roll \+ Agility** to get into position to kill it before it can regenerate, and then **Roll \+ Agility** again to line yourself up for the kill as usual. If you have a team member going for the kill after you’ve incapacitated a Titan, then they only need to make the one roll to line themselves up for the kill.  
When attacking a Titan’s limbs, **Roll \+ Technique**. On a 10+, you successfully incapacitate the limb. If your technique score is \+3, you can completely dismember the limb instead. On a 7-9, you fail to completely incapacitate the Titan, but do a major wound to it, likely slowing it down.

#### ***Hooking Onto Titans***

***When fighting Titans, priority one is to use your environment, not the Titan’s body.*** When trying to hook onto a Titan’s body directly, make a difficult **Roll \+ Agility**. On a failed roll, the Titan will grab onto your wire, and you will be left helpless. On a success, you must remake the check for every subsequent action you do, until you unhook from the Titan. A Titan doesn’t need to be already aware of your presence to grab onto the wire, as it can feel hooks going onto its body.  
If a Titan has noticed your presence, every time you want to move out of its line of sight, you must pass a regular **Roll \+ Agility**. On a failure, the Titan simply moves or turns around to keep track of you, and may even try to approach you.  
When moving right next to a Titan, whether it be running or ODM gear, the Titan gets a free opportunity to try to grab you, which is treated as a regular grab attempt.

#### ***Confusing Titans***

Titans are tunnel-visioned killing machines. When focused on a single target, all hope is lost in terms of defeating them. Your only chance against a Titan is to use the one advantage you have over it, your mind. By confusing a Titan with several targets moving fast enough, you can force it into revealing its weak spot. When you and another member of your team move to distract a Titan in a confusing way, **Roll \+ Agility**. You receive advantage for each team member trying to help you distract the Titan. On a success over a Titan’s **Roll \+ Mind**, you successfully daze it, and receive an advantage to getting into position before you strike to kill the Titan.  
To kill even a single Titan requires careful planning and teamwork. You need to use the terrain around you to your advantage, and have teammates draw Titans towards them so that a Titan may be injured, and killed. The only way to stop a Titan from trying to react to you hooking directly onto its body is to majorly injure it first, and have someone act as bait, as to distract it from noticing you while it’s focused on both the pain *and* another ally. To try to kill a Titan on your own is almost certainly suicide, unless you’re the best of the best.  
On a failed attack, a Titan will notice your presence, and focus on you, ignoring any other immediate distractions.

#### ***Killing Titans Without Maneuver Gear***

You probably won’t be able to. Killing a Titan without manoeuvre gear follows the same rules as normal, except for two key differences. The first one is that you cannot slice chunks out of a Titan, due to lacking the momentum, and the second is that only a titan-slaying blade can pierce its skin. The eyes and ears are still susceptible to guns and knives, if you can reach them.  
If you’ve managed to make your way to a Titan’s weak point, you must succeed in a difficult **Roll \+ Technique** to get your titan-slaying blade into the right spot, a difficult **Roll \+ Body** to actually force it to move in the way you want, and then a difficult **Roll \+ Agility** to successfully slice out a chunk of it faster than it can regenerate. If you fail any of these rolls, you fall off of the Titan, and your blade is stuck inside of its nape. You may **Roll \+ Body** to try and hang on. On a 7-9, you can retain the hilt, but the blade snaps off, and on a 10+, you manage to keep your grip. You must make this roll again every time the Titan moves, and must **Roll \+ Body** if you wish to take your sword out. On a 10+, you get the sword out, and on a 7-9, the blade snaps.  
Alternatively, you can blow up a Titan’s weak spot with a cannon or explosive, which would kill it.

### *Abnormal Titans*

Abnormal Titans, mechanically, work in the exact same way as a regular Titan would. There’s no difference between an Abnormal Titan and a regular Titan, other than its behaviour. Because of this, when an Abnormal Titan is used, it’s entirely up to the GM to decide how it may be different. It may be faster than a regular Titan, or it may just act and think in a strange way, such as targeting larger groups of people instead of doing what a Titan usually does, which is targeting the closest person.

## Fear

The world of Attack on Titan is a scary place. When seeing a Titan for the very first time, seeing a Titan eat a comrade (every time), or anything else that would completely shake a man’s soul, **Roll \+ Heart**. On a 7-9, your next roll is difficult. On a 6 or lower, you are completely frozen in fear, until you’re snapped out of it. If you roll a 9 or lower when seeing a Titan for the first time, you must **Roll \+ Heart** every time you see a Titan until you roll a 10+.  
When snapping a comrade out of a state of fear, **Roll \+ Duty**. On a 10+, the player is completely recovered, and can act again. On a 7-9, they can follow you and act somewhat, but will automatically fail any roll until they have a minute to rest, in which case they will snap out of it. On a 6 or lower, you fail completely and someone else has to try, or they must be brought somewhere completely safe and had roughly a half hour (or equivalent) to calm down.  
When instilling fear into the heart of an NPC, **Roll \+ Duty**. On a 10+, the NPC is completely frightened, and will do as commanded. On a 7-9, they’re afraid, but might lash back, and might hesitate to follow orders. On a 6 or lower, either you completely failed to frighten someone, or you’ve frightened them so much that they see the situation as having no choice but to attack you, at the GM’s discretion.

## Hand-To-Hand Combat

Whether it’s a man-sized Titan, or just an asshole at the bar, sometimes you’ve gotta put up your fists, swords, guns, or what have you. Before a fight, if both parties know it’s about to begin, **Roll \+ Agility**. Whoever has the highest result may act first. Otherwise, if taking your opponent by surprise, you may act first immediately. When fighting with your fists against another person, the attacker must **Roll \+ Technique**, while the other must **Roll \+ Agility**. (Note, blocking and dodging while unarmed both use agility, but parrying with blades may use Technique instead. For the sake of simplicity, this will be referred to as “Evasion.”)  
If you are below half of your maximum consciousness (rounding down), every roll you make in combat becomes difficult.  
If the attacker rolls higher than the recipient, they still must pass a regular check. If the attack is a failure, (6 or less), the attack doesn’t do any damage to the recipient, presumably not hitting hard enough or just missing altogether. The same goes for if the recipient rolls higher than the attacker.  
If the defender’s stat used for evasion is \+3, and they roll a 10+ while beating their opponent’s attack roll, they may perform a counterattack of a free action while still attacking normally on their turn. While doing a counterattack, you can choose to throw your opponent to the ground.  
A successful hit on your opponent will result in a minor injury, and after you’ve done an amount of injuries equivalent to or higher than their **Body \+ Heart \- Your Body**, subsequent hits will take away 1 point of consciousness, but not any major injuries. This is different, however, if the attacker is Titan-sized or has a \+3 to Technique or Body, in which case, subsequent attacks *will* do major injuries on a complete success (10+).  
If the attacker is using any sort of weapon (that isn’t a crude improvised weapon, like a chair or a club), then rather than following the consciousness rules of a fistfight, you do wounds as normal. A 7-9 that beats your opponent's evasion does a minor wound, and a 10+ does a major wound. If the attacker rolls a 10+, and the defender rolls below a 6, then the attacker with a weapon may opt to do a crippling wound.  
If you roll a successful hit against your opponent, and deal an injury to them, you can try to go for a second attack, with a difficult attack roll. If you fail this attack, it leaves you open, and your opponent gains advantage on trying to hit you. This does not stack, and you can only get a combo of two hits at a time (after a counterattack) before your opponent gets a chance to react.

### *Attacking a Restrained Opponent*

When an opponent is restrained or incapacitated, **Roll \+ Technique**. If you are unarmed, you can hit as normal with no chance of the opponent dodging. This means that, theoretically, you could continue dealing consciousness damage until you beat someone to death. With a weapon, you deal wounds as normal. This means that for an execution, you want someone with a high technique, or it might be a brutal and messy affair.

### *Disarming an Opponent*

When fighting an armed opponent, you may make an attempt to disarm them. When trying to take the weapon out of an opponent’s hand, do a contested **Roll \+ Technique**. If you roll higher than your opponent, and roll a 7-9, you manage to get the weapon out of their hand. On a 10+, you manage to gain control of the weapon as well. On a 6 or lower that’s still higher than your opponent’s roll, you manage to get the weapon out of their hand, but take an appropriate minor wound on the limb you used to disarm them.  
If you are being attacked by someone with a weapon, if you roll higher than them and a 10+ on your defensive roll, you may **Roll \+ Technique** to disarm them. The same rules as above apply, however, you may make a free attack while disarming them if you roll a 10+, such as striking them as you grab the weapon, and then act normally afterwards as well. The rules for striking someone as you disarm them are the same as normal, however you gain a **\+1 Advantage**, and your opponent’s defensive roll is considered difficult. If you roll a 6 or lower to disarm your opponent, they get a free attack action with their weapon.

### *Fighting Dirty*

When fighting an opponent, we can’t expect everyone to play fair. When performing a dirty trick to catch someone off-guard, like attacking when someone isn’t expecting it, or throwing sand in their eyes, your opponent must **Roll \+ Mind**. On a 7-9, you gain a **\+1 Advantage** to your next attack roll. On a 6 or lower, you gain a **\+2 Advantage**.  
When performing a dirty trick to cause more pain than expected, such as kicking your opponent in the testicles, roll your attack as normal. Then, if it connects, your opponent must **Roll \+ Heart**. If your body or technique (depending on the attack) is a \+2  or higher, this roll is considered difficult. On a 7-9, your opponent loses an extra point of consciousness immediately. On a 6 or lower, your opponent loses a point of consciousness, and is prone. This does not stack, however, so you can’t just smash someone’s nuts continuously to stop them from ever acting.

### *Wrestling*

When wrestling an opponent, both fighters must **Roll \+ Body**. The one with the lower roll is pinned to the ground, grappled, or otherwise unable to move, until he wins another roll contest of Body. On a 10+ from the defender while still lower than your opponent’s Body roll, you are still pinned to the ground, but can still punch your opponent, or hit them in a similar manner. You can also use a weapon, if you have access to one. These attacks can be dodged or blocked as normal.  
When wrestling, you may also choose to strangle your opponent. This follows the general suffocation rules until you stop or your opponent breaks free.  
When attacking an opponent, after your hit has landed, you can choose to **Roll \+ Technique** or **Roll \+ Body** to try and throw them to the ground. On a 10+, you can wrestle your opponent starting with the upper hand (having them pinned to the ground, unable to act), or choose to stand where you are. If you choose to stand where you are, your opponent will lose consciousness equal to their own body stat. On a 9 or lower, you fail to properly throw your opponent, and both of you fall to the ground. This can be done on any attack, including a counterattack.

### *Being Prone*

When you are prone on the ground, you must **Roll \+ Body** to stand. If you are below half of your maximum consciousness, you must **Roll \+ Heart** as well, and pass both rolls. On a 10+, you manage to stand with no issues, and on a 7-9, your next roll is considered difficult. Whoever rolls the highest Agility is able to act first. If one person is prone, and the opponent is not, then the person on the ground has a chance to roll to stand up before the opponent can act, but the opponent gets a free action once that roll has been made.

### *Firefights*

During a gunfight, turns are taken in a similar manner to a fistfight. You can spend an action to shoot, reposition yourself, or reload. When shooting, **Roll \+ Technique**. If the person being shot at has a \+3 in Agility, then they can make a difficult **Roll \+ Agility**, as they’d have the reaction time to try to get out of the way. If the Technique roll fails, or the Agility roll is higher *and* also above 10, then the bullet misses. When your line of sight is in some way impaired, or something else would make it difficult to fire a shot, the Technique roll is Difficult. Shots cannot be dodged if you have a majorly wounded leg.  
In Titan World, there’s a varying level of technology throughout the regions. The island of Paradis generally has armaments of single-shot rifles, and muskets, whereas the Empire of Marley has semi-automatic handguns and rifles. When running a firefight through turn-based combat, a single-shot rifle and musket can be fired once every two turns, as every other turn must be spent reloading. On a difficult **Roll \+ Technique**, the musket or rifle can be “speedloaded” to be loaded and shot on the same turn, however, on a failure, the turn is wasted, and the gun must be loaded for the next two turns as opposed to the usual one.  
Ammunition is not tracked in terms of a clear number. Instead, the amount of ammunition a player has is a more abstract concept, determined by the GM, similar to the amount of gas that they have remaining. Generally, it’s recommended that you give your players a number of “Magazines”, and take one away after each fight, maybe multiple if they’re reckless with their shooting.

#### ***Cover***

When you are completely in cover, you cannot be shot by anyone that the cover blocks line of sight from, and you cannot shoot anyone that could not shoot you. In a firefight, you can use your action to peek from cover and shoot. This will leave you exposed for one turn, but returning to cover is a free action.  
The state of cover is up to the GM, who may decide that it can be destroyed after sustaining many shots, so cover itself can still be targeted by a combatant.

#### ***Carrying People***

When in a firefight, bodies are an object like any other. When carrying someone to drag them, **Roll \+ Body**. If that person is conscious, and willing, add their body modifier on top of your own. If they are unwilling, they may contest your roll using the rules for wrestling. If you roll a 10+ without being fought off, you can drag someone with no penalty. On a 7-9, you gain a \-1 to Agility until you let go of them, and on a 6 or lower you cannot move them. On a 10+ to drag someone, you can move right away, but on a 7-9, you must wait until your next turn.  
If using someone as a human shield, **Roll \+ Body**. If they are conscious, they may fight back as written above. On a 7+ without being fought off, you can use them to absorb attacks from your front until they receive a crippling wound to the chest, but you cannot use your weapon or perform any other actions unless you have rolled a 10+.

#### ***Group Firefights***

Very rarely is a firefight between only two people. In a case like this, there are rules to keep things as balanced as possible. At the start of combat, each combatant must **Roll \+ Agility**. On a 7-9, they may dive within a few metres of their position, and on a 10+ they can position themselves anywhere on their “side” on the battlefield. On a 6 or lower, they must stay where they are. This is intended to give players a chance to get into cover before a fight.  
Then, an “initiative order” of sorts begins with each group of combatants. Each group will choose one member to **Roll \+ Agility**, and whoever rolls higher has priority in their actions. On their turn, the group can do the following.

* Have **one** **member** make an attack, move, or do anything else considered to be an action, such as First-Aid.  
* When using ODM Gear, moving and attacking can be done on the same turn.  
* Have **all members** reload their weapons. (A weapon can only be reloaded when the entire group is reloading.)

**You can return to cover as a free action during your turn, even if you are not the one who is acting that turn, as long as you did not leave your cover on that turn.**  
Each group can decide who among them may act during a fight, and it’s up to the combatants to think logically and tactically about how they act. **Being out in the open is a death sentence.** Titan World is a balanced system when fighting other humans, but only if you’re smart.

# Luck

Luck is a very important part of Titan World, it’s the little control that you have over the chaos. When playing Titan World, the party (group of players) has a shared pool of “Luck points.” For a more difficult game, the pool would be one point per player, and for a more forgiving game, it’d be two points per player.  
This shared luck pool can be used by any player, at any time throughout the game. Luck can be used to re-roll any roll made by the player spending a luck after the roll has been made, or it can be used to add 1 to the roll.  
Luck is regained at the end of each mission.

# Your Duties as A Soldier

***“We will use any means necessary to ensure that the missions and our efforts to take back this world are not Abandoned.” \- Erwin Smith***  
As a soldier, you’ve been trained in many things. From the art of kissing ass, to firing hunks of steel at Titans, there’s a lot to know. This section will cover everything your character may have in their toolkit.

## Riding Horses

They say a dog is a man’s best friend, but a dog won’t carry you for miles on end, under the harsh sun. The horse is the soldier’s greatest means of transportation aside from the 3D Maneuver Gear itself, but it’s equally as challenging to get proper use of.  
When getting on a horse, **Roll \+ Duty**. On a failure, you can’t get the horse to obey you, and it’s frightened in some way. Every following check on this horse will be difficult, until you can calm it down with a difficult **Roll \+ Duty** check.  
While riding a horse, you may **Roll \+ Duty** to perform a difficult manoeuvre in some way. This could be speeding beyond your horse’s usual running speed, or weaving between various obstacles.  
When dismounting your horse, **Roll \+ Agility**. On a failure, you fail to dismount gracefully, and fall onto the ground. Your next roll will be difficult.

## Firing Cannons

Loading, aiming, and firing a cannon in total requires three actions. First, you must load your ammunition. On a regular black powder cannon, this can be done without a roll. On more advanced artillery, **Roll \+ Duty**. Then, you must aim the cannon. This requires a **Roll \+ Mind**. Following this, **Roll \+ Technique** to properly fire it while keeping the aim steady.  
Throughout this, each action takes up a “turn” in the sense that whoever you’re going against can act after each action. If your target moves after you’ve aimed, you must aim again.  
Multiple people can cooperate to fire a cannon, this means that with three people, you can load, aim, and fire all on the same “turn”, giving your opponent no chance to react.  
If a Titan is hit with a cannon, a limb of the aimer’s choice is incapacitated. If the aimer chooses to, they can call to aim for the nape, but this must be done before they or the firer rolls. If the aimer or the firer rolls a 9 or lower after the nape has been called as a target, the shot misses.  
The head can be chosen as a target to incapacitate by the aimer, with a regular roll as usual. If it is hit, the Titan’s head is blasted off with a crippling wound. It is still alive, but cannot eat, see, or hear until its head regenerates fully.  
If a human is hit with a cannon, they take a crippling blunt wound on a limb of the aimer’s choice.

## Commanding a Crowd

People aren’t necessarily going to trust just any soldier who walks by. GMs should keep in mind that in this section, it’s recommended to give the players appropriate advantages as a reward for good roleplay. When trying to calm down civilians or appear more trustworthy, **Roll \+ Duty**. This **CANNOT** be done on player characters. On a 10+, the civilians are ready to do what you say, so long as you’re reasonable. They may still have their hesitations if you ask something absurd of them. On a 7-9, people refuse to do what you say, and may do the opposite, and on a 6 or lower, people may become violent or aggressive. Some people are in no position to listen to the players, such as an active enemy. In that case, it’s advised that the GM not make the players roll. The same goes for if someone is in no position to refuse the players, such as a hostage.  
When trying to get supplies you may need from your higher-ups, **Roll \+ Duty**. On a 10+, they will do their best to get you anything you request, though may not be able to get everything. On a 7-9, they’re hesitant, and want to meet you halfway. On a 6 or lower, they completely ignore your request, and insist you go without it.

## Planning Ahead

Sometimes, it’s important to gather information before a mission. Because of this, you can do your best to do research. When gathering information, or coming up with a plan, **Roll \+ Mind**. Note that this can only be done when you are in a position to gather information on a subject, and cannot be done otherwise. On a 10+, you may ask the GM two specific questions, and the GM must be truthful. They have the right to refuse to answer a question if it wouldn’t make sense for the character to find out the answer, in which case the player must ask another one, but the GM cannot lie. On a 7-9, you may ask the GM two questions, but the GM must give a believable lie as the answer to a random one. On a 6 or lower, you may not ask any questions to the GM.

## The *opposite* of Your Duties

Sometimes, you need a break on the job. When drinking, **Roll \+ Body**. On a 6 or lower, decrease Agility and Technique by one. On a 7-9, increase Heart by one, and decrease your choice of Agility or Technique by one. On a 10+, increase Heart by one.  
When sobering up, **Roll \+ Duty**. On a 7+, you lose the effects of inebriation. When someone is helping you, the helper should make the appropriate roll, then take the average. Add both rolls and divide by two, rounding down.  
When taking a night to rest, **Roll \+ Heart**. On a 10+, you regain all points of consciousness, and your minor wounds are healed. On a 7-9, you only regain all consciousness. On a 6 or lower, you cannot sleep. This roll is automatically failed if you have any major wounds, but does not apply to crippling wounds, so long as they’ve been treated if they’re cutting wounds, or amputated if they’re any other kind of wound.

# The Blurry Line Between Man and Titan

***“To surpass monsters, you must be willing to abandon your humanity.” \- Armin Arlert***  
All Subjects of Ymir have the innate ability to become a Titan, even if they do not possess one of the nine powers. This power can only be unlocked through a complex chemical formula, which nobody quite knows how to synthesise, other than Marleyan scientists.

## Players becoming Mindless Titans

When a player becomes a Mindless Titan, that player character is gone, and a Mindless Titan is created in its place. That Titan acts like any other Titan would, and should be treated by the GM as a regular Titan NPC. If that Titan eats a Titan Shifter, then the player will receive their character back, as a Titan Shifter. This will also heal any wounds the character may have had, and may be used as a last resort to save somebody’s life.

## Titan Shifters

Titan Shifters have the completely unlocked ability to become a Titan, one of the nine original powers. Depending on who this character ate, decides their power. A Titan Shifter can also eat other Shifters, to gain their combined power.  
Titan Shifters have several unique abilities. Their most prominent one is that they can turn into a Titan form at will. If they are losing blood in any way, and have a clear goal pictured in their mind, lightning will strike the earth where they stand, and they will emerge in their Titan form. When trying to transform, **Roll \+ Heart**. Use the following table when you’re new to using your Titan Form.

| On a 10+ | You successfully transform into your Titan Form, and go on a rampage, attacking anything in sight, human and Titan alike. Those close to you can calm you down on a difficult Roll \+ Duty. |
| :---- | :---- |
| **On a 7-9** | Your Titan form partially forms, satisfying only one single task, such as blocking an attack from an incoming Cannon with a skeletal hand. Describe what you are trying to do, and the GM will decide how the Titan form appears. |
| **On a 6 or Less** | You fail to transform into your Titan form, and nothing happens. |

**Use the table on this page when your character has more adapted to their Titan form, and knows what they’re doing. When using this table, you may choose what combination of your acquired Titan powers you wish to use.**  
**If you have ONE crippling wound, you can transform halfway, and the same table applies, but your Titan is completely immobile, skinless, and missing its body from the waist down.**

| On a 10+ | You successfully transform into your Titan Form, with no issues. |
| :---- | :---- |
| **On a 7-9** | You transform, but your Titan form is weaker than it usually would be, in some way up to the GM. |
| **On a 6 or Less** | You transform, but you’re weaker, and any items you had on you are now completely destroyed. |
| **On a 2 or Lower** | You transform, but your Titan form is incredibly weak. All of its physical stats (Body, Agility, Technique) are reduced to \-1, and it cannot use its regular abilities. It’s also very frail, with a maximum consciousness of 1, and about half its regular height. |

As a Titan, you gain several advantages. Any Body checks against regular-sized opponents are an immediate critical success, and you can perform hand-to-hand combat against Titans up to the size range of your new Titan Form. (Note, anything that isn’t Colossal is considered “regular-sized” to the Colossal Titan.)

### *Titan Shifter Regeneration*

When wounded, a Titan form will heal at the same rate as a regular Titan, and they similarly can only be knocked out through slicing out their nape, or receiving a crippling head blow from a similar-sized opponent, which would result in the shifter being torn out of their Titan form.  
Titan Shifters outside of their Titan form will not heal quite as fast as Titans. They’re still affected by consciousness, but will heal minor wounds in a matter of minutes, at the GM’s discretion. When resting, Titan Shifters may **Roll \+ Heart** to turn a major wound into a minor wound, with the same rules as regularly healing a minor wound.  If they have a crippling wound anywhere on their body, they are unable to transform. Crippling wounds can only be healed on a Titan Shifter, and will be healed once the mission has ended, or they’ve had a full night to rest.  
When dismounting your Titan Form, **Roll \+ Body**. On a 7+, you manage to get out with no issue. On a 6 or lower, you are stuck inside, and immediately fail the first state of Absorption. When dismounting your Titan form, your consciousness is reduced to one, and you must wait for it to regenerate before you can transform again.

### *Absorption*

Slowly, but surely, a human will be absorbed into the nape of their Titan. Humans within Mindless Titans are instantaneously absorbed, and can only be retrieved through eating a Titan Shifter. Within shifters, however, they are slowly absorbed over time. After a decent amount of time has passed, and you’re beginning to grow tired, **Roll \+ Heart**. On a 7-9, your limbs are completely fused with the Titan’s nape, and must be severed to cut you free. You are completely fine at this point other than that, however, and can continue as normal. Each subsequent roll of this, at the GM’s discretion, should apply a negative modifier of one, similar to the rules for dodging.  
Once you’ve become completely exhausted and you’ve been in your Titan Form for hours, **Roll \+ Heart** after every action you take. On a 7-9, you fly into a maddened rage, and attack everything around you. On a 10+ you manage to hold on just a little longer. On a 6 or lower, you fly into a maddened rage, and you are completely fused into your nape. At this point, slicing your nape would only kill you, and the only way to salvage your Titan power is to have another Titan bite your nape off.

### *Killing Mindless Titans as a Titan Shifter*

Killing a single Titan is easy for a Titan shifter, but a horde is difficult to deal with. When fighting Mindless Titans, you may only target one at a time, as you usually can with fighting. Because of this, other Titans may be drawn to you, and will attack you while you are fighting. Rules for dodging a Titan apply as normal, meaning you will take an Agility penalty for each subsequent dodge, until you’ve had a second to catch your breath, even while you’re fighting.  
A bite from a Titan will begin as a minor cutting wound. Three bites will become a major cutting wound, and five will become crippling. If you have no chance to rest, it’s easy to be swarmed and killed by even just five Titans at a time if you’re not careful.  
A mindless Titan will instantly be killed by most attacks to its head, assuming that it hits the nape.  
For the Armoured Titan, the amount of damage a Titan bite does is halved, so it takes six bites to become a major wound, and that will result in the armour being broken.

### *Being a Beacon for Titans*

When you are on the battlefield, Titans may act abnormally around you. In your Titan form, Titans that are close enough to you will immediately target you rather than humans that may be closer. In your human form, it is less extreme, however Titans will likely favour you out of a group and try to attack you specifically.  
By letting out a loud roar, you can intentionally call Titans to your location, possibly to use as a weapon after dismounting, or to allow them to eat your Titan body so you may escape a trap.

### *You Are No Longer Yourself*

Over time, you will gain the memories of previous Titan Shifters that had your power in particular. Along with this, you will adopt aspects of their personality. You’ll start to think as a combination of your own thinking, and all previous shifters. The GM may opt to change some of your mental stats (Mind, Duty, Heart) to reflect this, but that’s entirely optional, and this is mainly meant for roleplay purposes and finding out new information.  
You will also begin to share preferences with your previous shifters. An example of this is Armin Arlert, who fell in love with Annie Leonhardt likely for the sole reason that he ate the previous Colossal Titan, who was also in love with her. Another example includes Eren Jaeger, who began to tie his hair up like Laura Tybur after eating her.

### *The Curse of Ymir*

As the original Titan Shifter died 13 years after acquiring her power, all Shifters will die 13 years after gaining their power, no matter what, so that none may surpass her. This can theoretically be bypassed by having all nine Titan powers, but it’s unknown for certain.  
If a Titan Shifter dies without being eaten, their power will be passed on to a random Eldian newborn infant. If there are none, it will go to the next to be born. If the Shifter has multiple Titan powers, they will be split up among multiple children, as opposed to remaining as one.

# Running the Game

***“What is the point if those with the means and power do not fight?” \- Eren Jaeger***  
As the Game Master, your job is to make sure things are running smoothly, and everyone, *including yourself*, is having a good time. Generally, there’s a checklist of things you should think about when preparing a game to be run for your players.

* **Who will they be meeting?** It’s good to come up with characters in advance for your players to meet, that way, it will make the world feel more alive if the players don’t think every NPC is being made up on the spot.  
* **What is their goal?** It’s very important to have a clear goal set for your players, and it should be straightforward and simple. Complications can be introduced later, but the first thing should be a goal. It can be anything from “Rescue this hostage”, to “Map out this area beyond the walls”, or even just “Assassinate this man.”  
* **Where will it take place?** It’s a good idea to map out where you think your players will be playing the game itself. If they’re going to be fighting in the interior, then you should come up with a basic layout of the city they’ll be in. If they’re gonna be beyond the walls, map out some simple terrain.  
* **How difficult will this mission be?** When coming up with characters for the players to fight, it’s important to think about how difficult you want a mission to be. A single Titan can be enough to kill a group of people if they aren’t careful, so you must be very cautious of how forgiving you are in this deadly game.
